# HeyHood — Good things. Close by.

## Your community already has the answer. Now you can find it.

### The problem: a thriving local economy, hidden in plain sight

Inside a large gated community, someone is making fresh breakfast, teaching Hindi, offering makeup services or organising a weekend workshop. A neighbour may be heading to the airport at exactly the same time as you. The supply exists. Finding it is the problem.

Today, that knowledge lives in fragmented WhatsApp groups, old messages, referrals and notice boards. A resident must know whom to ask, join the right group and hope someone responds. New residents start almost from zero. Even when they find a name, prices, timings and availability may already be out of date.

Providers face the other side of the same problem. A talented home cook or tutor must keep posting to stay visible. A good local technician cannot easily see which community needs their service or whether enough households want it to make a visit worthwhile. Residents miss useful options; providers miss business.

**The missing layer is a current, searchable understanding of what a community can offer—and what it still needs.**

### The solution: one familiar chat window for your hood

HeyHood gives residents a simple, WhatsApp-like conversation with Aapta, their community guide. They describe what they need in English, Indian languages or mixed-language everyday speech:

> “Any idli available this morning?”
> “Need Hindi tuition for my child.”
> “Anyone going to the airport tomorrow night?”

HeyHood matches the need against relevant offerings, people, schedules, rides and plans in that community. It makes the provider, resident name, flat, price and available timing easy to understand, then routes the resident into the appropriate next step.

For commerce, residents can open the provider’s page, explore offerings and place an order request. Providers manage their menu and availability, accept or decline orders, update readiness and track handover. Payments stay directly between the resident and provider. During transition, providers can also record orders received through calls, WhatsApp or paper in the same order book.

### One matching engine. Several everyday needs.

- **Buy nearby:** food, snacks, groceries, flowers and gifts.
- **Find a skill:** tuition, beauty, tailoring, repairs and home services.
- **Go together:** airport rides and reciprocal school runs.
- **Make it happen:** workshops and service days that need enough sign-ups.

The same resident can be a seeker, seller, organiser or ride partner. The interaction changes; the community context stays connected.

## From local need to local opportunity.

### The advantage: community context that compounds

A generic directory knows business names. HeyHood’s potential advantage is understanding a community: who offers what, where they are, when they are available, which needs recur and which matches work.

Accurate, refreshed context makes the next answer more useful. The moat is earned through resident trust and successful matches—not simply an AI interface or a growing search log.

### A missed search can become a business opportunity

Three or four residents search for car washing and find no suitable option. HeyHood flags the pattern for review. The operator checks the gap, confirms household interest and aligns a service window. An outside provider can then serve that concentrated demand.

**Search → qualify the gap → bring in supply → fulfil the need → measure repeat demand.**

The same approach supports bicycle repair days or AC servicing. Searches are sourcing signals, not confirmed bookings. Across neighbouring communities, this can become a demand-led distribution channel for local service businesses.

### Who pays—and why

Residents find relevant options. Providers gain discoverability and a route to qualified demand. Revenue opportunities to validate include:

- **Provider subscriptions:** ongoing visibility and business tools.
- **Labelled promotions:** relevant offers with measurable responses.
- **Service activations:** concentrated community demand; later, partner booking or referral arrangements.

The pilot must prove incremental business and willingness to renew.

### Where HeyHood fits

WhatsApp owns the habit. MyGate and NoBrokerHood have community reach and commercial surfaces. Justdial offers broad discovery; Urban Company offers established service workflows.

HeyHood’s opening is current, community-specific matching and a repeatable way to fill proven gaps. It complements society operations; security, accounting and complaint ticketing stay outside its scope. Better outcomes must prove the differentiation.

### Start with density. Earn the right to expand.

The pilot focus is MyHome Tridasa and MyHome Sayuk in Hyderabad—approximately 6,500 flats combined. Start with frequent food and everyday-service needs, supported by classes. Rides and plans deepen community utility.

Prove repeat usage, useful matches, accepted and completed requests, provider renewals and gaps successfully filled. Then replicate across nearby communities. Longer term, authorised diagnostics, wellness and pharmacy integrations could extend the same chat-led experience; these are opportunities, not existing partnerships.

**HeyHood makes the economy inside a community visible—and turns what is missing into the next local opportunity.**
